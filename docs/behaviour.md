# Behaviour contracts

## Tracking

Completion is canonical; editions may retain their own seek positions. Each accepted change appends an event and updates the per-user projection in one transaction. Duplicate external events do not increase play count. An explicit rewatch may add a play. A no-op cannot undrop an item; genuinely changed progress or a manual state change can. Movie completion or one completed regular episode establishes meaningful progress and removes the ordinary unstarted watchlist presentation. Saved intent remains available for To Watch, In Progress, Complete and Dropped filters.

A partial rewatch retains canonical watched status and its existing play count while exposing the new position through Resume and Continue Watching. A completed position starts a fresh playback at the beginning; another play is counted only when that playback reaches completion.

Show and season completion use regular episodes by default, excluding specials. Explicit episode identity is independent of display order. Bulk watch/unwatch records individual canonical changes. Destructive or contradictory sequences require acknowledgement, and imported contradictions are retained for review instead of silently overwriting Coast history. Ratings are 0.5–5 in half-star steps; null removes a rating.

## Metadata

Resolution is user preference → administrator override → current primary provider snapshot → fallback provider data. An administrator lock suppresses the user preference for that field. Provider snapshots are never mutated by overrides. Local media-server metadata is preferred by default; the administrator may enforce TMDB-only metadata. English and the user's selected region are defaults, original titles remain searchable, and artwork choices are presentation preferences. Each snapshot retains source and update time. Partial search/discovery snapshots preserve richer detail fields; an explicit complete refresh may clear obsolete fields. Detail pages refresh missing or seven-day-old regional TMDB details. Collection members are loaded on a relevant collection details page and ordered by release date.

## Provider capability contracts

Adapters implement only supported capabilities: authentication, catalogue, metadata, availability, playback, scrobbling, tracking/list exchange and requests. Coast application services own canonical mapping and permission checks. Native fetch, boundary validation, deadlines and constrained responses are shared infrastructure; provider-specific behaviour stays in each explicit adapter.

Jellyfin scans checkpoint pages, publish per-user availability and reconcile disappearance only after a complete successful scan. An interrupted scan never removes the rest of a library. Trakt is explicit import/export after optional account linking; Coast remains authoritative. Imports keep source identities, survive disconnects and are idempotent. Seerr is linked to its destination media-server instance and enforces its own request permissions as well as Coast policy.

## Playback

The title player survives route navigation. Each hero owns its inline trailer video, with its own source, position and play/pause state. The Coast companion provides crop metadata before playback. Trailers apply it automatically, and title playback starts fitted to the full frame. The Home button in the navigation bar brings Coast forward and changes to a Play icon that returns to the current video. Navigating to a different page pauses personal video playback. Artwork remains visible until the trailer and its crop are ready. Trailer pause or end returns to artwork, and a hero-to-details handoff keeps the trailer position. Trailer audio starts muted. Explicitly unmuting either player mutes the other.

Full playback uses a Coast-owned session, obtains Jellyfin PlaybackInfo, evaluates every source and applies administrator bitrate/delivery policy. Starting a different title closes the previous session; resuming the same title reuses its video and session. Native-compatible direct streams are preferred when allowed; Jellyfin supplies transcoding and HLS, with hls.js only where native HLS is unavailable. Tokens remain server-side behind authorised relays. Edition and source/quality are distinct selectors.

During playing title playback, five seconds without pointer, touch or keyboard activity fades both playback controls and the header/navigation. Activity reveals them again. Paused playback, post-play, errors, active menus/dialogs and keyboard-focused controls keep the chrome visible. There is no Hide controls button. The persistent playback bar remains available while browsing; resuming returns to the title at its current position. Its compact glass layout shows artwork and title above a thin progress line. Hovering the centre or focusing its range control replaces the metadata with elapsed/remaining time and expands the scrubber. Touch devices expose the scrubber directly. A drag previews the position and commits one seek on release; keyboard adjustments seek immediately. Scrubbing keeps the controls visible. Quality contains Resolution, Audio track and advanced video and audio settings. The toolbar puts Volume, Quality and Subtitles in that order. Hover over Volume or use the arrow keys to open the slider. Click to toggle mute. Moving the slider unmutes playback. On touch, tapping also opens the menu. Music queues are in Volume’s advanced options. The filled Stop button sits next to Play/Pause. Start synced sessions from Parties. Double-click or double-tap the video surface to toggle the companion crop. Press C to toggle it with the keyboard.

The shared page shell keeps an opaque black canvas outside the animated route content. It covers the full page on Library, Search and other screens without a hero; hero pages expose only the hero area, using the same responsive height token as the hero itself. Content gutters, shelf gaps and short pages remain black during navigation.

Playback sessions begin prepared. Accepting subtitles allows the source to load; the video’s actual playing event starts tracking and external reporting. Cancelling before that point stops the prepared session without viewing history or scrobbles. The client sends playback reports in order, and each session transition, canonical progress update and configured outbound intent commits in one transaction. Provider delivery remains asynchronous. Playback failure creates an administrator diagnostic and a concise user message. No FFmpeg runs in Coast. Subtitle defaults, preferred languages, the optional pre-play prompt, manual selection, provider labels, seek, crop and post-play use the persistent control layer. The crop toggle combines companion crop data with viewport fill. Subtitles remain anchored to the viewport.

## Outbox and notifications

Actions have no default expiry. A worker claims work using PostgreSQL locks, preserving order per user/connection. New pending edits can replace obsolete edits with the same compaction key. Retry uses bounded exponential backoff, not dropped state. Permanent failures notify the initiating user; successful recovery resolves transient failure notices. Users can inspect/cancel pending work. No Redis or browser-offline queue is needed.

Notifications are persistent per-user inbox records with silent, normal or persistent presentation. Administrative unsilenceable notices override user silencing. Polling refreshes inbox, jobs and requests; WebSockets are unnecessary.

It ranks visible, dated activity from accepted friends over the last 30 days by distinct friends, then latest activity. Episode/season activity rolls up to shows, music tracks to known albums, and each friend counts once per root work. Category privacy and experimental gates apply before ranking.

Friends opens to the roster, with privacy-aware profile avatars and lazily loaded overall taste scores for accepted friends. Insufficient data and private insights are distinct states. The full Activity view lives at `/for-you?section=activity`, reached from the home Activity shelf; Friends contains the roster; comparisons open from taste scores. Recommendations are handled through notifications rather than a Friends tab.

### Account activity status

The existing account menu starts with activity status (Online, Away, Busy or Invisible), with a Share activity checkbox for live presence, then a separator and Profile. The checkbox preserves feed privacy and restores the previous live presence audience when re-enabled. Online automatically becomes Away after five minutes without interaction or media playback, and Offline after 90 seconds without a heartbeat. Manual Away and Busy persist but do not keep disconnected accounts online. Invisible appears Offline and hides live media presence from other users. Presence privacy applies to the friends roster, activity avatars and media friend heads. Online, Away and Busy use the shared status dots; Offline has no avatar dot. Status is read in batches; browsing heartbeats run every 30 seconds with overlapping writes coalesced. No new scheduled job or page is used.

### Notification inbox

The header bell and Friends buttons open persistent popovers over the current page. Both use the dark material’s CSS blur fallback, capped at 560px wide and 640px high with a 16px viewport inset. Outside clicks, Escape and Close dismiss them without a modal backdrop or scroll lock. Standard category segments sit in the pinned header beside options and Close, with no separate panel title. Notifications use submenus for read status, type, medium and date, followed by Refresh and Mark all as read. Filters apply before bounded cursor pagination; marking read uses the selected filters and database snapshot. Friends has Friends, Requests and experimental Parties segments, paginated before presentation, with username-based requests and existing accept/decline/cancel actions. Friend cards show avatar, username and shared live activity or presence; profile, taste comparison, activity and removal live in each card’s menu. Backgrounds use shared live artwork or the friend’s visible profile background. The standalone Friends and Notifications inbox pages are removed; taste comparisons remain at `/taste/{friendId}`. Profile backgrounds default to the latest known dated watch, listen or game play visible to the viewer. A selected fixed background remains selected until its owner chooses Last activity; unknown dates, future events and private activity cannot supply the automatic background.

Entries resolve local media artwork, availability, actor privacy, current request/session state and relevant Jobs destinations in batches. Friend requests live in the Friends panel; recommendations use the ordinary media actions, including Watchlist. Each notification appears individually in date order and is marked read when visible in the open notification panel. Offscreen notifications remain unread. Marking or dismissing a notification affects only that visible owned record. Privacy changes and expired invitations are revalidated when reading or acting. Service notices whose owned outbox job has succeeded or been cancelled are excluded from the inbox, toast list and unread counts; original notices and job history remain stored. Pending, running and failed jobs retain their notices. Older records remain accessible through Load older notifications; no provider calls run while browsing the inbox.

Friend recommendations appear in their own lazy Recommendations row on For You, with Watching/Playing/Listening and independent availability filtering. The expanded shelf uses `/progress?view=recommendations`. Only received pending recommendations from current friends qualify; duplicate recommendations for a work share one card and identify their senders. Deleting a delivery notification leaves the recommendation intact. The ordinary Watchlist action saves the title; there are no separate Save recommendation or Dismiss recommendation menu actions. Recommendations are recipient-only and filters/counts are applied before page hydration.

Experimental parties live in the Friends panel’s Parties segment. The joined party stays in an edge-to-edge footer outside the panel scroller across every segment, with a transparent participant/action area and artwork confined to playback. A host can create an idle party, invite accepted friends, and start video or music through the existing player. Media changes, queue changes, seeking and playback commands stay in the same party. Each participant prepares their own accessible compatible source; party membership never grants server access. Stopping host playback leaves an idle party; ending the party closes it for everyone. Invitations expire with the existing 24-hour session lifetime. Source preparation failures stay visible with Retry playback, without removing the member from the party.

Party options reuse the shared nested Button menu: playback controllers, readiness, buffering policy, invitation permissions, host disconnect behavior and music queue permissions. Joined members can inspect sync status, resync, adjust a local playback offset and choose whether playback continues after leaving. Settings are enforced server-side under the room lock; membership and readiness never grant access to media. Automatic advancement remains host-owned. Host disconnect defaults to waiting; continuing is limited to one minute, and ownership transfer requires an eligible joined member. Personal offset and leave preferences are stored per user in the current browser.


## Feature controls

Settings → Features lets each person opt out of permitted features while retaining history, lists, credentials and privacy choices. In Settings → Feature availability (Feature permissions in the redesign), **Allow** controls whether an account can use a feature and **Enabled** edits the member’s current preference. Membership restrictions and feature dependencies also apply. New installations leave experiments and Media card layouts off. Existing choices and grants persist. Personalise appears in the account menu when Media card layouts is active. The Settings redesign is off by default and its account permission controls access to the entire redesigned interface. Media detail panels remain standard signed-in navigation.

Social governs Friends, reactions, shared activity/recommendations and party participation. Public profiles retain their independent site-access and privacy rules. Presence Off stops optional browser reporting and provider live observations for that person; history imports and administrator server-stream inspection are separate. Playback/Reader Off retains manual tracking. Sharing still requires its installation policy and account grant. API availability never creates a token; token/share revocation and saved-request cancellation remain management operations.

Affected active use stops at its next safe application boundary. Withdrawal cancels unsent optional work through the durable queue, closes owned readers and affected sessions, and preserves previously accepted progress without inventing completion. Stopping only party coordination can leave standalone playback running. In-flight remote effects and already buffered/direct media bytes cannot be recalled. Disabling features is not a comprehensive network-isolation setting. Other tabs refresh their visible choices through the existing session/navigation lifecycle; server commands and subsequent resources enforce current policy independently.


For You includes Recently added and Requests shelves using the shared lazy horizontal loader and expanded grid. Recently added ranks accessible titles by their Jellyfin added date when available and falls back to Coast’s original index date. A show moves up when a newer episode becomes accessible to the viewer. Requests use Available, Processing, Accepted, Pending and Declined in that order, with Available selected by default. A movie/show filter applies within each segment. Request cards use the activity-style artwork and caption, with a status indicator in the reaction position. Administrators see all requests with the actual requester avatar/name/date header. Ordinary users see their own requests without that header. Request scope is enforced on the server and refreshed when viewer identity or role changes.

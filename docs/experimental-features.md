# Experimental features

Administrators manage feature permissions per account in **Settings → Feature permissions**, or from a member’s details in **Accounts**. **Allow** exposes the feature in the member’s **Settings → Features**. **Enabled** is the member’s current preference. Either the member or an administrator can change it. Revoking access hides the toggle and preserves the saved choice.

Experiments default off on new installations. The migration retains existing grants and choices. The **Settings sidebar** experiment is off by default for every account. It opens Settings over the current page and uses the same settings data as the original page.

- **Music:** album/track browsing, listen tracking, audio playback and Jellyfin music imports.
- **Gaming:** game discovery, playthroughs/sessions, IGDB and Steam integrations and Steam jobs.
- **Books:** Open Library discovery, explicit additions and private page-based reading progress; no media server required.
- **Comics:** Comic Vine issue discovery and private reading progress; an administrator API key and non-commercial use are required. See [reading experiments](reading.md).
- **Parties:** synced video/audio sessions and invitations. Video parties work independently of Music and Gaming; music playback also requires Music.
- **Planning & calendar:** scheduled plans, reminders and upcoming releases.
- **Rich toasts:** show the current notice on a glass card with avatars and artwork when available. Turning it off restores standard toast cards.
- **Taste matches on cards** adds a personal match percentage to media card subtitles, including For You and Library. The viewer needs Personalisation enabled and enough history for a score.

Dynamic For You, personalised recommendations and media detail panels are standard features, without experimental switches. Detail panels also have no personal opt-out; their underlying media and commands retain ordinary permissions. Personalised horizontal rows load as you scroll, combining cached provider suggestions and separate genre, tag, creator and medium-specific taste signals. Music and Gaming still require their respective switches.

Disabled media are hidden from rows, search, social activity and public API results before pagination. Direct pages and APIs are gated independently. Disabling a feature preserves saved relationships, history, games, connections and sessions. Film/TV and ordinary Jellyfin connections remain available.

Feature grants and preferences apply to subsequent requests without a restart. Saving refreshes the current tab; other tabs update through navigation or session polling. Site access, registration and disposable playback links live separately under Access & registration.

## Performance and verification

Experiments follow the [performance methods](performance.md) used by the rest of Coast. Disabled features remain independently gated; feature switches do not create another scheduler or provider-fetch path.

- For You and Discover fetch one ordered set of eligible row definitions per visit. When the last visible shelf enters the viewport, the feed reserves the next two layouts using standard shelf skeletons. The second shelf waits for the first to settle before loading its cards. Confirmed empty shelves collapse, and the feed stops at the end of the definitions.
  For You interleaves personalised genre and title-based “More like…” rows with Popular with friends rows for movies, shows, games and music. Popularity uses privacy-filtered data. Each visit shares an interest-weighted ordering seed across its row requests. API requests without a seed use a stable per-user daily order. Content revisions refresh visible shelves while preserving their positions and order. Changing viewer or enabled media starts a new visit.
- Personalised recommendations use up to 100 positive works from 300 recent distinct personal works and candidate selection at 2,000 before scoring. Counts and stable ordering precede 60-card page hydration. Fresh per-user taste scores supplement provider matches and bounded genre matching; this is not an exhaustive catalogue ranking or a predicted rating.
  Genre titles reflect evidence (for example “Because you enjoy drama” or “More drama shows to explore”). Movie and show rows filter by kind before the candidate cap; their full-grid links preserve this distinction. Popularity titles say “Popular … with friends” rather than implying friends are currently playing or watching. Dynamic genre shelves score matches to their named genre, with a genre-specific seeded tie order rather than repeating the same broad recommendation ranking in every shelf. The seed follows the title link into the full grid and remains fixed across card pages and refreshes. Title-based rows restrict evidence to the selected personal work and preserve that work in the full-grid link; another user's work cannot supply personal evidence. Row definitions draw from at most 300 recent personal works. Broad personalised recommendation rows retain the existing recommendation ranking.
  Row requests use the normal Shelf resource and cancellation. The feed opts out of document scroll anchoring while mounted so adding reserved space leaves the viewport in place. Other shelves retain their existing 300px lazy-load margin.
- Planning limits scheduled plans to 100 per user, paginates reads and hydrates each distinct work once per page. Reminders use the existing outbox. The plan dialog fetches its friends only when planning a party and cancels requests when that context closes.
- Rich toasts reuse the notification window and shared artwork loaders. With the experiment off, the toast query omits attachment data and skips avatar and media hydration. With it on, context is batched for at most 50 notices, one card is mounted, and only the current temporary card runs a dismissal timer.
- The detail overlay loads its component only on first use, keeps the detail payload in raw state and reuses its enhancement promise until that payload changes. Tracking revisions refresh an open overlay without reopening a loading panel; writes and session refreshes use SvelteKit’s state-preserving refresh while a shallow overlay is open (ordinary pages retain targeted dependency invalidation); stale responses are aborted and permission failures clear the old detail data.
- Party heartbeats prevent overlapping requests and reject results from a previous room generation. Active sessions keep their two-second heartbeat even while browsing; changing this cadence requires multi-account playback evidence. There is no heartbeat request without an active room.
- Music, Gaming and Reading reuse shared Collection/card loaders and provider request pacing. Reading joins the existing Search, Library, Discover, Continue, Next, favourites and lists, with independent Books/Comics gates, actual category privacy and page-based progress. Reading availability uses only that user’s observed Jellyfin files. Local PDF/EPUB/CBZ/CBR/CB7/CBT readers, bookmarks, state history, cached taste signals and format-specific party locations reuse the shared experience; there are no separate browsing pages. Music and Gaming retain their durable jobs. Charts remain isolated, lazily rendered demo data; see [chart preview](chart-preview.md).
- Temporary material interaction sheen coalesces pointer movement into one layout read per animation frame. Leaving, updating or destroying the sample cancels pending work; reduced-motion and touch retain static behavior.

Focused verification covers feature gates, resource cancellation, content revisions, material frame cleanup and synced client stale-response handling. Isolated database suites cover recommendations, Upcoming, plans, independent gates, onboarding and party policies. Type/build checks complement these fixtures; they do not establish live provider or multi-device playback acceptance.

The October experiment pass also exercised a disposable browser fixture: lazy dynamic rows, on-demand detail opening and a tracking write that retained the overlay and refreshed its controls. This is local fixture evidence, not live provider acceptance.

## Core interface parity

The movie/show page and detail overlay share `loadMediaDetails`: saved data appears first, followed by optional metadata/collection enrichment and request destinations. An outage retains saved details and the same recovery notice. The overlay uses the shared `MediaDetails` composition and `DetailOverlay` container, with medium-specific data and commands. `MediaDetailRows` renders provider-neutral Insights, Reviews and Credits contracts; unsupported capabilities are omitted. Saved game and music cards use their canonical full-page loaders in the overlay too. The panel is the standard signed-in detail interface; no experiment or personal toggle disables it. Direct URLs and modified/new-tab links retain the canonical full-page routes.

Upcoming and recommendation shelves use the shared medium segments, header arrows, availability button (recommendations/dynamic rows only), loading/empty behavior and grid pagination. Upcoming has no availability filter because it describes future releases. Full-grid filter/page changes update the URL; empty settled rows hide without an experimental notice holding them open. Dynamic genre rows retain their fixed medium and use the usual availability control.

Upcoming, personalised recommendation and dynamic genre row titles use the shared Shelf heading link to open their full grid on For You. For signed-in browsing, these linked titles open a continuously appended panel view. Links preserve medium, genre, kind, visit seed and applicable availability filters; Planning destinations remain behind their experimental toggle; dynamic and recommendation grids are standard features. Grid titles do not link back to themselves.

Planning uses Watching/Playing/Listening/Reading segments, filtering both counts and items before pagination. Plan writes use the shared mutation refresh once. Both connected-service and persisted music detail pages reuse the existing music/card action menu rather than a separate social-only menu. Manual listen/album logs live in that menu; track Continue is shown for recorded progress, while albums retain sequence continuation. Medium-specific activity remains concrete: plays, listens and watch events are not interchangeable. Approved party cards and controls retain their existing layout.

Parity verification includes a local browser fixture for the shared detail hero, Upcoming’s page clamping and lack of an availability filter, and Planning’s shared medium selector. Enrichment unit fixtures verify immediate saved content, collection refresh and independent request destinations during a metadata outage; isolated database fixtures verify medium-filtered Planning and experiment gates. No production/provider configuration was changed.

## Personalised dynamic rows and provider suggestions

Dynamic definitions use at most 300 recent distinct root works. Episode/season activity contributes to its show, track activity to its known album. Favourites and positive ratings carry more weight than completion, partial progress or watchlist entries; drops and low ratings reduce genre affinity. A work contributes once, so a history import or repeated listens cannot swamp other interests. Recent interests get a modest boost. Negative-only genres do not generate fan rows. Saved-only rows avoid claiming enjoyment.

Row ordering uses a visit-seeded weighted shuffle with a penalty for consecutive rows of the same medium or selection type. Strong interests usually lead; occasional exploration rows must come from genres that co-occur with established interests in the shared catalogue. They are labelled as exploration. Appended rows keep their positions, and genre/kind/reason filters follow the heading into its grid. This remains a heuristic, not an inferred certainty about someone's taste.

Dynamic screen and game rows also choose between posters and landscape thumbnails using that visit seed and the row's stable key. Every card in a row uses the same format, which remains fixed during loading, refresh and pagination. Music retains square artwork. These formats use the shared Shelf presentation options and existing lazy artwork queue.

Broad personalised recommendation rows join the weighted shuffle for each enabled medium with positive personal evidence. They reuse the recommendation source's provider matches, taste scores and exclusions. They use the same paired reservations and sequential card reads as the other dynamic shelves. Their medium remains fixed in the row; the full grid retains the usual medium controls.

Row wording lives in the shared `row-titles.ts` store: eight variations for established genre interests and six each for consumed, saved and exploratory genre rows; five variations each for liked, consumed and saved title-based rows; three friend-popularity variations and five broad personalised recommendation variations. Selection uses the existing visit seed plus row context, so wording does not randomly change during refresh, paging or loading. Genre and related-title grids share the same functions and seed with their row headings. Saved/exploratory wording avoids invented likes or consumption, and popularity wording does not imply friends are currently watching, playing or listening. Genre aliases keep names such as science fiction, role-playing and R&B readable. Main task headings such as Continue, Next and Activity remain explicit.

TMDB movie/show recommendations, IGDB similar games and authenticated Trakt recommendations are refreshed through the existing outbox and provider request lanes. Each integration has a Recommendations job with pause, interval and Run now controls. Shared refreshes process at most six seed titles per run; each title contributes at most 20 suggestions. Trakt reads at most 40 suggestions per medium for one account per run. Cache replacement happens only after that set succeeds, including valid empty results; failures retain prior sets for retry. Trakt cache reads require a connected account with the matching generation, and stale workers cannot replace a new account's set. Imports add verified shared metadata without creating Collection or tracking state.

Provider matches receive the strongest ranking contribution; fresh personal fit and capped local genre matching supplement them. Dropped, consumed, already saved and poorly rated candidates are excluded as applicable. Page and scroll requests read the database, never call provider recommendation APIs. Shared seed sets refresh after a day; the configured job interval determines batch cadence. Music uses local genre and artist signals until a suitable recommendation provider exists. Account-specific Trakt recommendations refresh at the configured interval. Paused jobs retain previously cached data.

### Taste profiles and verified game variants

**Settings → Jobs → Coast → Taste profiles** uses the existing durable outbox, with pause, interval and Run now controls. A partial unique index admits one active `taste.refresh` job. Each run considers at most ten stale users. The worker streams all retained canonical works in 300-work pages. Ranking retains up to 300 recent seed works and 500 candidate works per medium. Normal batches follow the configured interval (60 minutes by default). Deferred users and full batches continue after a minute, and unchanged profiles rebuild weekly.

Coast builds one private reference for each enabled medium. Movies, shows, music, games, books and comics have independent histories. A direct show or album rating takes precedence over its children's ratings. Otherwise their median gives the parent one vote. Repeated plays, multiple episodes and duplicate provider observations cannot multiply the number of distinct works. For movies and episodes, the profile pairs Trakt and Jellyfin watches at identical timestamps and retains distinct watches from each service. State-only counts contribute when event history is unavailable. Accepted Jellyfin counts can also represent older plays for which no dates were supplied. A rated or favourited work can contribute feedback without a recorded play.

The normal item scorer combines personal ratings and tracking. A movie or show can receive a further adjustment when independently rated titles support a pattern involving its creative team, personal agreement with TMDB audiences, credits and story, or a person and company. Games, music, books and comics use their own available features. Missing evidence can leave a title without a percentage. The median rating in the profile is a descriptive statistic.

With personalisation enabled, **Admin → Calculate taste score** inspects the signed-in viewer's match. When card scores are enabled, selecting a card percentage opens the same explanation. It shows the personal estimate, the features that contributed to it, the strength of the evidence and any final adjustment. A score of 50% is neutral. Friend profiles compare the two viewers using their sharing settings.

Taste enriches from existing provider metadata reads. IGDB supplies game collections and supported languages alongside genres, game modes, perspectives, franchises and companies. Steam contributes playtime and ownership, with IGDB supplying catalogue details. Jellyfin rich music reads can add tags, studios and verified writing and composition credits. Open Library work subjects include places, people and periods, and its indexed editions supply languages and publishers. Comic Vine issue details can add concepts, credited characters, teams and places alongside the series and creator roles. Provider fields appear when returned, and ordinary or partial observations preserve previously enriched fields where supported. Existing items acquire new fields during their normal rich metadata refreshes.

The worker prepares the lookup values and confidence weights once per medium. Rewatches, repeat listening, completed rereads and completed repeat playthroughs contribute a separate signal across affinities. Unrated returns are compared with the owner’s returns on other titles with comparable metadata. For rated or endorsed titles, one deliberate return can reinforce a neutral or positive opinion while preserving the original rating and the distinct-work count. Well-supported completion and rating rates can strengthen a preference relative to the owner's other titles in that medium. Completion evidence covers movies and ended shows with a known episode census. A show still in progress can provide an unfinished observation when the user last watched it at least 90 days ago. Games, books and comics use recorded completion or a drop after recorded progress. Music uses plays and deliberate returns. An unusually long game can contribute a small extra signal when the owner has other games with known playtime. Short games incur no penalty. The Taste segment reads the saved summary and pages one dimension on demand.

Returns carry more weight when they are unusually common for an affinity. A genre or subject can also combine with another affinity, such as a theme, credited creator or franchise. The worker learns a return-only tie-breaker when the return rate differs from both features individually and each comparison has sufficient evidence. The rule also works without ratings. It orders recommendations with the same final ranking score and leaves the displayed taste percentage and confidence intact.

Cards reuse their visible scores until the published profile changes. The server calculates a title against the saved personal reference and current local metadata. The scoring service can share this work across a batch of up to 72 titles. Episodes, seasons and linked tracks use the show or album as context. Verified credits on the specific item can change its score. Box sets and comic volumes sample up to 24 distinct local members across release order and show how many matched. At least two titles and half the sample must have a score.

Profile and item reads check the owner’s current source permissions. A scoring-model change queues a fresh profile, and concurrent personal imports defer publication. Friend comparisons use visibility-filtered evidence. Rating agreement includes relative ordering when both people have rated five shared titles using at least three distinct values each. See the [scoring review](audits/2026-11-20-full-history-taste.md) for measurements and limits.

Verified IGDB `parent_game`/`version_parent` links let playtests and variants borrow main-game presentation and taste metadata. Their IDs, names, Steam ownership, achievements and playthrough history remain separate. Existing Steam/IGDB mappings missing feature data are checked again on the next owned-games import. A name-only match is not sufficient, and an unlinked playtest remains its own record.

Unknown imported activity dates remain unknown. Acquisition, listening and playing records use distinct “date unavailable” labels; an import timestamp is not substituted for when the user actually consumed the item.

API references: [TMDB movies](https://developer.themoviedb.org/reference/movie-recommendations), [TMDB shows](https://developer.themoviedb.org/reference/tv-series-recommendations), [IGDB similar games](https://api-docs.igdb.com/), and [Trakt's official recommendation schema](https://github.com/trakt/trakt-api/blob/master/projects/api/src/contracts/recommendations/schema/response/recommendedMovieResponse.ts).

See [shared detail panels](design.md#shared-detail-panels) for the standard presentation, edition selection and nested navigation.

## Featured heroes

Admins can use a screen item's context menu → Admin → Feature on For You, or remove it from the same menu. Features are a site-wide ordered list, capped at 20, and keep the exact movie, show, season or episode selected. Missing/deleted selections are skipped; an empty usable list resumes the established personal fallback. Automatic fallback still promotes episode/season candidates to their show. Feature mutations refresh the current route through the existing settings/tracking dependencies; other viewers receive the current list on their next route refresh/load.

All shared heroes use five seconds of artwork, the complete available trailer, then five seconds of artwork before advancing. Without a trailer they show ten seconds of artwork. Multiple supplied items loop in order; a single item stays on its artwork after the cycle. Hidden/offscreen/covered heroes pause their deadlines and retain the remaining time; focused controls pause automatic advancement, and reduced-motion preferences suppress automatic playback/rotation. Manual arrows and playback/mute controls remain available during artwork and trailers, without a page counter. Play/pause controls the artwork deadline as well as the video; during trailers, pause fades artwork over the paused video and resume fades it away. Item changes crossfade decoded artwork and fade copy/actions together while navigation stays steady. Mute choices persist into the next trailer. The first row after a shared hero has no extra outer top gap; the hero keeps its own internal spacing.

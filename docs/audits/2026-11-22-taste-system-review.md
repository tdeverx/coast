# Coast taste system review

Reviewed the working tree on 22 November 2026, including the unpublished selection and viewing-habit changes. This is an audit of current behavior. The tuning ideas below are experiments. The controlled examples use synthetic histories, and the ranking figures come from the previously recorded account replay.

## Fix first

| Priority | Finding | Adjustment and acceptance check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A creator or studio can earn an exemption even when the viewer disliked every joint title. In a controlled history, six one-star films were the only films watched in the rare category. They all came from a studio the viewer loved elsewhere. The fitter granted that studio a full exemption, cancelling a 56-point deduction on an 80-point input. It counts joint selections and reads the studio's overall affinity. [Selection training](../../src/lib/social/taste-selection.ts#L134) | Keep compact counts of joint enjoyment, drops and deliberate returns beside joint consumption. A supported joint dislike should veto the exemption. Test the mirror cases of loving and disliking the same studio within one genre, plus an unrated repeat viewer. |
| 2 | The 17% catalogue prevalence ceiling creates a discontinuity. With 180 identical historical choices and zero selections of a niche tag, 85 tagged titles among 500 catalogue opportunities produced a 30% match. At 86 the same candidate scored 93%. [Rule admission](../../src/lib/social/taste-selection.ts#L93) | Taper influence using prevalence and uncertainty. Perturb sample counts and metadata near every admission boundary and measure the largest single-title swing. Keep sparse and unknown evidence neutral. |
| 3 | A weak descriptive feature can suppress a strong independent preference. Forty low-rated genre titles and forty high-rated actor titles gave a candidate matching both a score of 59%. Adding a tag rated once at one star and once at five lowered it to 31%. The tag's own affinity was neutral. The weakest matched descriptive dimension sets a ceiling for every secondary dimension. [Scorer](../../src/lib/social/taste-profile.ts#L524) | Anchor the secondary ceiling to supported descriptive evidence. Run a test that adds neutral or weakly evidenced metadata to the identical candidate and inspects both the score and each dimension's influence. |
| 4 | Classifying a repeat as deliberate can erase a learned combination. In a controlled 240-title example, ordinary repeats produced genre–writer tie-breaks of about ±0.73. Marking those same repeat events deliberate erased the pair signal. The accumulator omits strong returns from the returned count while still counting the eligible pair as consumed. [Accumulator](../../src/lib/social/taste-profile.ts#L341) | Keep the verified return event for combination fitting independently of how much vote weight it contributes. Upgrading the quality of the evidence should retain a supported pattern while preserving one vote per title. |
| 5 | Scope and strength can be lost before training. A single episode or track rating can supply the entire show's or album's rating when there is no direct parent rating. Its parent metadata gets trained. An explicitly paused, long-idle ended show can also supply a failed completion where its episode census is known. [Canonical evidence](../../src/lib/recommendations/interests.server.ts#L119) · [Completion](../../src/lib/recommendations/interests.server.ts#L143) · [Feature attribution](../../src/lib/social/taste-cache.server.ts#L253) | Carry the source scope, child coverage and credits relevant to that observation. Keep one parent vote, with support appropriate to the evidence. Treat a pause as an unresolved outcome. Verify a guest episode, a single collaboration on an album, a season rating and a pause followed by resumption. |

## Where the product uses different evidence

- **For You admission and ranking.** The warm shortlist takes up to 500 works per medium. Provider membership, positive feature matches and release date determine who gets scored. Future works can occupy slots that released recommendations subsequently cannot use. Genres for this warm pass are pooled from recent interests across media. The live row also deducts three points for each genre present in the viewer's recent negative titles, independently of the lifetime profile. Apply surface eligibility before spending the candidate budget, scope seeds to their medium, and compare the independent genre deduction with removing it. Record which held-out works entered consideration and where they finished. [Warm shortlist](../../src/lib/social/taste-cache.server.ts#L311) · [Row ranking](../../src/lib/recommendations/query.server.ts#L28)
- **Profile wall and admin explanation.** The wall preview uses the raw feedback affinity, the detail list can substitute a selection-adjusted value, and a feature with zero historical appearances can affect a title while having no entry in that list. Name and expose enjoyment, chosen versus expected, and final title contribution separately. A strong avoidance-only case currently receives no title score if ordinary affinity evidence cannot pass the initial gate. Evaluate whether showing that reason alone with limited confidence helps. [Profile wall](../../src/lib/profile/PersonalTaste.svelte#L44) · [Affinity detail](../../src/lib/social/taste-cache.server.ts#L62) · [Scoring gate](../../src/lib/social/taste-profile.ts#L581)
- **Profile overlap and friends.** The comparison page computes exact counts but samples up to 300 works per person for scoring. An episode and its series can enter that sample separately. The explicit title calculation uses another test for friend agreement. It samples up to 120 of the viewer's ratings per matching work kind, then looks for matches. Older shared ratings can fall outside it. Align the meaning of a shared work, intersect eligible evidence before taking a bounded sample, and keep visibility checks at read time. Report the personal score and any live friend adjustment distinctly. [Profile comparison](../../src/lib/social/insights.server.ts#L75) · [Similarity](../../src/lib/social/taste.ts#L51) · [Friend evidence](../../scripts/benchmarks/incumbent-friend-taste.ts#L41)
- **Choice and metadata across media.** A partial movie with only a position, a book started on page one and a game played for one minute have different admission behavior. One listened-to track can establish an album choice. Reading metadata can attach multiple sampled editions' languages and publishers to one work. Define exposure, deliberate choice and completion per medium. Attribute the edition or version actually used when known. Missing edition data should carry uncertainty. [Activity sources](../../src/lib/recommendations/interests.server.ts#L54) · [Album choices](../../src/lib/social/taste-cache.server.ts#L269) · [Edition features](../../src/lib/social/work-features.server.ts#L83)

## Organisation and performance

- Give lifetime evidence, recommendation seeds, score composition, and explanations explicit ownership. The same SQL builder currently contains full-history and recent-seed branches. Group each dimension's role and default influence in a named policy. Give the packed profile format named accessors so a correction to one tuple field is easy to verify across training, scoring and display. Preserve bounded batches, prepared references, and the existing atomic publication check. [Evidence query](../../src/lib/recommendations/interests.server.ts#L14) · [Dimension policy](../../src/lib/social/taste-profile.ts#L225) · [Reference encoding](../../src/lib/social/taste-profile.ts#L461)
- Separate the version of the user's learned preferences from the version of their recommendation shortlist. The newest public recommendation-set timestamp is part of every user's taste revision. Refreshing someone else's catalogue seed can therefore make a lifetime profile stale even though its evidence is identical. Also distinguish stale-but-readable scores from revoked evidence on every surface. Measure avoided rebuilds, queue delay and peak worker memory at growing history sizes. The history is paged, but the fitter retains fingerprints and a set of known works until publication. [Revision inputs](../../src/lib/social/taste-cache.server.ts#L17) · [Worker](../../src/lib/social/taste-cache.server.ts#L246) · [Fingerprints](../../src/lib/social/taste-selection.ts#L86)
- Make replay a repeatable project tool. Preserve split rules, model versions, denominators and aggregate results without committing anyone's raw history. Measure rated and unrated profiles, chronological and unfamiliar-franchise splits, shortlist recall, final top results, ties, abstentions and score bands. Add independent account or synthetic adversarial cohorts and uncertainty intervals. Compare against recency and popularity. The latest single-account replay improved unrated show pair ordering from 46.05% to 46.74% and slightly reduced movie performance. It excluded shortlist admission and had no negative Animation movie pairs. It cannot establish how often users actually like a title shown as an 80% match. [Recorded replay](2026-11-22-taste-selection.md) · [Prior chronological trials](2026-11-22-taste-comparisons.md)

## Additional adjustments worth testing

- **Personal opportunity.** Estimate what the person could reasonably have chosen, with separate library and discovery baselines. The sampler caps both pools and then discards their labels. Preserve pool sizes and test weighting their prevalence. If using actual exposure later, count distinct visible titles with bounded aggregates and distinguish a row rendering from a real opportunity. [Opportunity sample](../../src/lib/social/taste-selection.server.ts#L47)
- **Changes in preference over time.** Keep lifetime evidence and learn a smaller recent deviation only after several independent choices. Distinguish real activity dates from bulk import timestamps. Check that one weekend or one imported list cannot reverse a long-held preference.
- **Independent evidence within a franchise or series.** Seeing one actor across six unrelated films is stronger evidence of following them than six appearances in the same franchise. Retain both the franchise preference and the number of independent contexts supporting the person. The same principle applies to studios, authors, artists and developers.
- **Contexts appropriate to the medium.** Choice rules currently use genres as their only conditioning field. Ordinary book and comic imports supply subjects as tags and concepts as themes, and duplicate genres are removed. Test broad subjects as contexts for more specific topics or authors when genre data is sparse. Measure how many real catalogue works can support each pairing. [Reading import](../../src/lib/catalogue/reading.server.ts#L90) · [Feature cleanup](../../src/lib/social/work-features.server.ts#L126) · [Context selection](../../src/lib/social/taste-selection.ts#L51)
- **Positive and negative interactions.** Test whether an affinity pair consistently does better or worse than either feature alone. Fit corrections only on the residual after the individual effects, shrink low-count pairs toward zero, and keep bounded exceptions in the prepared reference. Current mechanisms already include fitted genre/topic terms, return tie-breaks and avoidance exemptions. Start with creator × theme, studio × subgenre and author × subject, and require a holdout improvement before expanding. [Current combination trainer](../../src/lib/social/taste-rating-model.ts#L77)
- **Personal pace and release opportunity.** Compare bingeing and release follow-through with the person's normal pace and the show's release schedule. A weekly episode watched every weekend can show as much commitment as a box set finished in two evenings. Account for local availability and date precision before inferring that someone fell behind. [Viewing habits](../../src/lib/recommendations/watch-habits.server.ts#L57)
- **Preference strength versus certainty.** Keep “likes the six they chose,” “rarely chooses it,” and “we know little about this candidate” as separately inspectable quantities. Test observed like rates within displayed score and confidence bands. Preserve one headline match score while making the reason for a low or withheld score visible.

The historical replay figures are prior measurements on one account. This audit did not rerun the live account replay, benchmark production latency or change application behavior.

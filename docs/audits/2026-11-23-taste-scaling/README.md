# What would make Coast’s match percentages more useful?

On this account, the best reason to lower a score was a repeated pattern in the viewer’s own weaker ratings. Changing the overall importance of genres, people or studios did little. The most promising experiment kept Coast’s current recognition of specific favourites, gave moderate ratings more weight, and allowed a bounded downward adjustment only when two independent parts of the viewer’s history agreed. It improves several important outcomes and still makes identifiable mistakes. It is a candidate for a side-by-side preview.

## How we judged the scale

We froze the real movie and show data used for the card preview. There were 765 movie and 566 show history roots, including 444 and 429 ratings respectively. We split by canonical title, withheld the title’s rating and all of its tracking evidence together, and fitted each candidate on the remaining history. This gave 444 movie and 428 show percentages. One show lacked enough evidence. The unchanged scorer reproduced all 1,746 rated and ratings-masked fold outcomes, including abstentions. An independent split kept whole connected franchises together. The show assignments in these two splits coincided. No imported rating was reliably dated before the chronological cutoff, so that replay tested the fallback.

For **ordering**, compare a randomly selected held-out title rated at least four stars with one rated at most three in the same fold. A correct order gets one point and a tie gets half. For **percentage accuracy**, Brier error uses whether the viewer gave four stars or more. This is one possible interpretation of the number. We also measured the actual distribution, the fate of five-star favourites and titles rated at most 2½, and the liked titles in the top 20. These checks catch changes that make a percentage look more varied without improving its usefulness. Rating calibration used independent training-fold predictions. The combined candidate gained 2.80 percentage points of movie ordering with a paired conditional 95% interval of −0.69 to +6.29. Its show change was +0.04 points, interval −1.41 to +1.49. Holding whole movie franchises out improved ordering from 76.54% to 79.93%, yet its Sci-Fi top 20 lost one liked film (16 to 15). The comparisons are exploratory and share one viewer and the same folds. An independent account or untouched future ratings would provide better evidence of generalisation.

## What dynamic influence actually did

1. **Change the weight of an entire family.** We learned how much genres, tags, credits and other categories predicted the viewer’s ratings using inner folds. Simple multipliers moved movie ordering from 75.96% to 75.96% and shows from 79.32% to 79.14%. Common genres and tags often give the same answer. Giving one family more influence leaves that agreement intact.
3. **Compare the nearest rated works.** A 16-neighbour content model gave rare shared details more weight than common ones and shrank predictions toward the viewer’s observed four-star rate when similarity was weak. Its signed adjustment took ordering to 75.57% for movies and 77.39% for shows. Blending it with a linear model helped some individual runs but also pushed the median for unseen non-Ghibli anime films from 31 to 54. The viewer already has evidence of selectively preferring Ghibli there.
4. **Change how much a star rating contributes.** The existing scorer applies distance from three stars to both the direction and strength of evidence. A 2½-star vote supplies just −0.0625 signed units before other adjustments. A square-root strength curve retains the direction and makes that vote −0.125, with a five-star vote still +1. The tested neutral strength was 0.25. Changing the raw profile accumulation produced most of the improvement in identifying high-scoring weak titles. Changing only the separate fitted rating correction did less. [Eight rating-weight alternatives and calibration trials](../2026-11-22-taste-rating-calibration.md)
6. **Calibrate or stretch the final number.** A nested logistic calibration gave better four-star probability forecasts for both media, but brought the unseen Ghibli median from 86 to 78 and cut five-star movies scoring 80+ from 59 to 16. Limits on the adjustment protected more favourites but retained some of the same cost. A monotone stretch by itself also preserves mistaken orderings. The meaning of the card percentage matters before optimising its spread.
7. **Use behaviour when stars are sparse.** We independently revealed 0%, 10%, 25%, 50% and 100% of training ratings. With no stars, score ordering was 64.73% for movies and 66.79% for shows. More influence from catalogue prevalence, completion and returns together moved those to 65.66% and 56.12%. The history supplied two repeated movies, six repeated shows and zero dropped roots. It offered limited evidence about what the viewer actively disliked. The current unrounded, confidence-aware ordering reached 70.72% and 69.12% in the masked comparison, useful for ordering within a list. Supervised candidates retained the existing scores with ratings masked. [Implicit feedback and confidence](https://yifanhu.net/PUB/cf.pdf)

## What changes during ordinary browsing

On 3,094 scored unseen shows in the frozen opportunity sample, the middle half spanned **75–85** today and **67–85** with the combined candidate. Median Documentary moved from 65.5 to 56. Median Animation also dropped from 78 to 71, even though more liked Animation titles made its top 20. That tradeoff matters for this viewer. On 4,190 scored unseen movies the middle half shifted from **76–82** to **75–81**. The six unseen Ghibli anime films retained their 86 median, and the 77 other unseen anime films retained 31. The movie labels included only seven titles rated at most 2½. The available evidence does not warrant forcing a broad spread on every shelf.

In an isolated Bun arithmetic benchmark, applying the two compiled models to 72 already-featured titles added 0.15 ms median for movies (0.49 ms p95) and 0.12 ms for shows (0.75 ms p95). The two sparse coefficient maps together occupied 25,876 bytes for movies and 14,055 bytes for shows as compact JSON. This measured 340 warmed batches per medium after 20 warm-ups. It excludes fetching and decoding the reference, metadata extraction, HTTP, database work and UI rendering. A background profile build could store both models once and card requests would reuse the existing batch and metadata. [Actual browser cost of the current card score](../2026-11-22-card-taste-preview.md)

**Recommendation.** Compare the existing score, the moderate-rating adjustment and the bounded counterevidence adjustment side by side in the admin breakdown before changing the card default. Evaluate on untouched ratings and another account with more low-rated movies. Preserve abstentions, sparse-profile behaviour, Studio Ghibli and strongly evidenced creators. Keep separate counts for incorrect high scores and genuine favourites moved below 80. In this preview, 80 is also where a card changes colour. The work above changed the experimental evidence only.

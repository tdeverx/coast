# Exploratory follow-up requiring the identical lead team and an actual dislike

The unconstrained residual test lowered some favourites because a related work fell short of an optimistic prediction, even when the viewer still liked it. This follow-up was chosen after inspecting the original whole-title results. Its settings were fixed before running the following comparison and it is not an independent confirmation.

Use the identical nonempty set of stable film director IDs or show creator/showrunner IDs. All independently scored works from that exact lead team in training, outside the target’s own franchise, must have received a verified personal rating of 2½ stars or less. A mixed, middling, liked or unknown team cannot generate a penalty. Use the mean of the donors' independent native prediction errors, weighting each franchise once. Lower an existing personal match only when that mean is negative. The two fixed variants require one or two independent donor franchises respectively. Do not re-fit a multiplier or tune the rating threshold. Report both green losses and newly red liked titles, how many unique creative teams benefit, and whether the dated and reduced-rating holdouts repeat the result.

All available direct ratings are checked for a veto even when the donor's independent native prediction is missing. A strictly chronological fold uses only dated ratings from earlier days. A residual itself still requires an independent prediction.

## Results

The one-donor rule moved four of 33 disliked shows under 50 on the whole-title test. Each had one other title from the identical show creator or co-creator team and nothing from that team that the viewer liked. These formed two reciprocal pairs. The primary and alternate franchise assignments repeated those same four titles. On the date test one of seven disliked shows moved from 76 to 48 using a rating that was already available. This personal signal needs no audience input.

There was a clear counterexample among films. A director had made one film that the viewer rated two stars and another they rated four. Withholding the liked film meant that the sole remaining vote was negative. The rule wrongly moved it from 80 to 30. It also appeared as a false red in one 50-rating mask. No five-star title changed score on any of the 32 panels. There were no held-out examples meeting the stricter requirement for two independently scored, exclusively disliked donor franchises. That version reproduced all baseline scores.

| One-donor rule | Dislikes below 50 | New reds among four-plus titles | Five-star scores lowered |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Whole titles | 4/40 | 1/613 | 0/125 |
| Franchises | 4/40 | 1/613 | 0/125 |
| Alternate franchise assignment | 4/40 | 0/613 | 0/125 |
| Earlier rating dates | 1/14 | 0/272 | 0/59 |

On two of the three 50-rating show samples a single disliked show moved below 50. All zero-, two- and ten-rating scores stayed as originally predicted. This exploratory result is a specific explanation supported by two teams, and the liked-film counterexample matters even though every five-star favourite was preserved.

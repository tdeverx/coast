# Recognising a sampled series the viewer moved on from

The candidate uses dated actions on other works. It compares titles the viewer started and then left while actively choosing alternatives with titles they returned to over several days. This tests whether that distinction carries information about the viewer’s opinion of similar, unseen titles. A missing watch, one completed film, a pause, a delayed release or an unavailable sequel does not establish disinterest.

For shows, require a credible watch of episode one or two, eight episodes released by the first credible viewing and 90 days of observation afterward. A sustained return means at least six distinct episodes over at least three credible days in that window. An early exit allows at most two distinct recorded episodes up to the current cutoff, plus credible activity on at least two other shows over at least three days in the next 90 days. Film anchors are the first watched title in a franchise with another later-release film already released by its first credible watch. A positive requires a separately dated watch of a later film within 90 days. An exit requires no other recorded family member by the cutoff and the same affirmative evidence of choosing other films. Once a later recorded watch contradicts the exit definition, the flag disappears. Repeated provider records, bulk timestamps and unreliable near-release clocks supply no positive event evidence. Episode and film release dates proxy opportunity; personal access at the time is unknown.

Compare the two nearest independent early-exit families with the two nearest sustained-return families. Similarity is the mean Jaccard overlap of nonempty genre, tag and creative-credit fields. The creative field contains directors, writers and creators. Audience scores, other users and popularity are absent. The target and its family supply no anchors. Whole test partitions including their timing and follow-on activity stay outside training. For an earlier rating-day test, only events strictly before that day can supply evidence. A weak association between having returned and user enjoyment must be measured before it can change a card.

To fit a personal correction, use independent, already frozen personal scores from inner family partitions and earlier rating days. Within the outer training set, each fitting example's behavioural score additionally excludes its entire inner family partition including subsequent activity used to establish other exits. Fit a line to the difference between observed star utility and the original personal prediction. Require at least 16 rated cases and a negative slope exceeding one standard error in magnitude. Subtract that standard error from the effect, apply the centred correction and round once to the card scale. Missing data or a masked rating history gives no adjustment. No target opinions or target activity enter its prediction. Keep the 32 original panels and report rating-mask coverage, pair order, strict dislikes below 50, five-star scores below their original green threshold and newly red liked works. Only a positive result on the release criteria would justify further serving work.

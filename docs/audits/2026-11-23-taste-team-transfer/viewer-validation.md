# Does this particular viewer have a track record of disliking another work by the same director?

After the first MovieLens 25M sample, register a further test on the second disjoint viewer sample before inspecting its labels. The strict same-team, similar-genre rule can predict a dislike only if it also previously worked for that viewer on other directors. Replay each viewer's training ratings chronologically. Require at least 25 earlier ratings, the same verified film identities and genres as the original external test, an unrelated previously disliked film directed by the same team, no earlier contradictory rating of that team outside the target franchise, and distinct rating days. Count an earlier opportunity even if it would have made a mistake.

Exclude all past validation events from the proposed target's own director team. Require at least two other director teams with an unbroken record of correctly anticipating personal dislikes, no historical four-star-or-higher counterexample, and at least half of validation outcomes disliked. A single successful other team is a separately reported sensitivity. Withhold the penalty for a viewer whose own experience supplies insufficient evidence. Compare eligibility before/after, subsequently rated dislikes and liked titles, changed percentages and red crossings. Held-out ratings never decide whether the proposal is admitted.

The first two disjoint samples yielded three and six eligible predictions under the strict rule. Keep that two-successful-team policy frozen and test on all remaining 14 hash buckets as a separate replication; do not tune it or combine its outcomes with the samples that motivated the replication. Stream the archive and reproduce the first two samples' coverage in the same pass.

## Results

The first group offered 177 exact-team transfers. Fifteen qualified with one prior successful team and three with two. The second group offered 192, with 24 and six qualifying. The two-team rule produced one newly correct red in the first group and zero in the second, where it turned one middling film red. The looser one-team rule put two liked films below 50 in the second group. Ratings used to validate the viewer's track record always predate that viewer's complete held-out block.

The untouched 14 buckets contained 38,015 eligible movie viewers with 4,015,155 held-out ratings. The original identity and genre criteria offered 2,813 opportunities. Of these, 675 had even one previous personal transfer check for a different director. The two-successful-team rule applied to 153 titles for 86 viewers. Of these, 104 were actual dislikes, 40 were middling and nine were liked. An accurate description of a selected group of titles does not by itself improve existing predictions for them.

Of those 153 predictions, the full adjustment changed 126. It moved six real dislikes, six middling films and one liked film from 50 or above to below 50. Another 112 adjustments lowered scores already below 50. The mean absolute error against personal star utility rose from 16.71 to 20.61 points. No five-star title qualified. Requiring two relevant donor franchises from the target's director as well as the two successful other directors changed eleven actual dislikes, each already below 50. It added zero threshold crossings.

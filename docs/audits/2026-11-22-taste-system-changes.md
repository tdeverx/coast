# Personal taste scoring and card loading

Coast trains each person's lifetime profile in the background and publishes a prepared reference for each enabled medium. The admin calculation, collection member scores and card batch lookup share that reference. The admin calculation also checks for currently visible friend evidence. The card measurements below use personal scores. ## When a creator can lead a match

A director or writer gets extra weight when ratings of 4★ or higher recur across independent works more often than in that person's other ratings. Three independent high ratings can start a pattern. Its strength grows with breadth and consistency. Several entries in a known franchise supply at most one independent high vote for this purpose. Partial evidence from a child rating carries less weight on its parent. Ordinary watches, drops and returns still inform the underlying affinities.

The worker stores the strength and supporting counts. Repeated low ratings for the same creator within a genre, tag or theme can withhold the extra weight on that combination. A writer-director shares the evidence across their roles. The admin breakdown shows the actual weight on the candidate and the count of highly rated works. [Creator trainer](../../src/lib/social/taste-creators.ts) · [Regression examples](../../tests/taste-creator.test.ts)

- On the copied account, seven movie directors and fourteen writers qualified. All seven rated Tim Burton films had at least four stars. Directing carried 35% of the weight on *Batman Returns*, the largest single signal. It already scored 89% with the ordinary creator weights and reached 90% with the personalised weight.
- Caroline Thompson's five ratings were all at least four stars across five independent works. Writing carried 25% on *Black Beauty*. It scored 89% without writing credits and 90% with them. Guillermo del Toro had five high ratings across three independent works. His writing preference lifted *Don't Be Afraid of the Dark* from 83% to 84% relative to ordinary weighting.
- In a controlled history with eight five-star works by a writer and thirty two-star dramas by someone else, a new drama scored 21% on genre alone, 55% with the ordinary writer credit and 78% with the demonstrated writer preference. The writer carried 66% of the blend. A separate test covers a viewer who repeatedly disliked that writer's dramas.
- The copied catalogue had a verified director or writer for only 37 of 566 shows in the account's history, including 27 of 429 rated shows. All 765 historical movies had at least one of these roles. The new TV importer reads aggregate crew from the existing TMDB request. It attributes a series role at 25% episode coverage, with two episodes required on multi-episode series. Individual guests keep their episode credit. A creator credit alone supplies no writing credit. Normal metadata refreshes enrich existing shows and a subsequent full taste rebuild uses them. The replay below used the existing snapshot. ## What else changed

- **Choice and enjoyment.** Library supply, discovery supply and historical choices inform the opportunity estimate. Prevalence tapers smoothly. Joint likes, dislikes and deliberate returns establish which specific exceptions deserve protection. Unknown metadata abstains. A genuinely unusual choice can stand on its own with limited confidence. Among the copied catalogue titles tagged anime, the 11 Studio Ghibli movies had a median match of 91%, compared with 37% for the other 78 anime movies and 40% for 120 non-Ghibli anime shows. With ratings withheld the same medians were 67%, 15% and 15%. These are estimates on scored candidates. [Selection model](../../src/lib/social/taste-selection.ts)
- **Lifetime evidence.** The trainer counts canonical titles, carries coverage and credits from rated children, distinguishes exposure from deliberate choice by medium, and attributes known reading editions. Pausing leaves completion unresolved. Release habits use known timing and local availability. Only dated evidence can change a recent trend. A burst of rewatches must exceed the viewer's other comparable returns in the same period. Retained history still contributes to the lifetime fit. [History projection](../../src/lib/social/taste-history.server.ts) · [Viewing habits](../../src/lib/recommendations/watch-habits.server.ts)
- **Combinations and explanations.** Joint effects describe combinations above or below the individual affinities. The scorer preserves verified returns as combination evidence and measures independent creator support. The profile can display an avoided feature even with zero appearances. Profile comparisons and optional friend adjustments canonicalise shared works and check permissions when read. [Score composition](../../src/lib/social/taste-profile.ts) · [Profile wall](../../src/lib/profile/PersonalTaste.svelte)
- **Refresh and recommendations.** A changed public recommendation set can refresh the bounded shortlist without rebuilding the user's lifetime evidence. Candidate admission filters future releases before the cap and uses medium-specific seeds. The measured changes target the personal match. For You still uses its own additional row-ranking signals. Its historical shortlist recall and the effect of its separate negative-genre rule were outside the personal-match replay. [Warm scores](../../src/lib/social/taste-shortlist.server.ts)

## Account replay

The comparison holds ingestion, user, eligible catalogue and held-out works constant and swaps the preceding local scorer for the new scorer. Each score is evaluated on unseen works within its fold. Pair ordering counts how often a title rated at least four stars outranks one rated at most three, with half credit for ties. The table reports confidence-adjusted ordering. The standalone percentage is also evaluated separately. [Replay tool](../../scripts/taste-replay.ts)

| Individual works | 78.73 → 78.89% | 69.34 → 69.64% | 77.74 → 78.08% | 52.31 → 52.33% |
| Whole franchises | 79.96 → 80.65% | 73.78 → 73.90% | 77.74 → 78.08% | 52.31 → 52.33% |
| Future works by first verified watch | 64.09 → 64.43% | 64.09 → 64.43% | 55.50 → 58.19% | 55.50 → 58.19% |

The work folds scored 444 of 444 rated movies and 428 of 429 rated shows. The chronological splits held out 111 movies and 107 shows. No historical star ratings could be placed before their cutoff, so both chronological modes used the same training evidence. The series franchise metadata yielded the same split as individual works. The benchmark covers one account. Small differences fall within the uncertainty of the sampled titles.

Rated movie likes in the top 20 rose from 18 to 19. Shows fell from 17 to 16, as did Animation shows within their top 20. Withheld-rating Animation results held at 17/20 movies and 10/20 shows. Standalone percentage ordering on movies fell from 73.13% to 71.84% with ratings and 62.32% to 61.78% without them. Isolating just the new creator weight improved rated movie ordering from 78.77% to 78.89%, with the same 19 top-20 likes. It made no measurable change for shows on the historical credit coverage. An 87% average match in the top show score band corresponded to 255 four-or-five-star titles among 345. The percentage expresses fit, and its numerical value overstates the observed like rate in this band.

A separate synthetic replay used 36 profiles across six media, with 900 titles per profile. On the 30 structured profiles, rated ordering rose from 71.39% to 71.80%, and ordering without ratings from 61.43% to 61.47%. The six random controls remained near chance. These constructed histories test model behavior. They do not establish precision on additional real accounts.

## Cost and verification

- The final 24- and 72-card trials use actual card projections, scoring, and JSON serialization. They compare the same IDs under all-hit, half-hit and all-miss score cache conditions. The benchmark verified card order and every returned percentage. No per-card SQL, provider request or UI rollout was introduced. Browser rendering and concurrent traffic need a separate measurement before rolling out scores across browsing surfaces. [Timings and method](2026-11-22-taste-card-loading.md)
- In an isolated query fixture with 7,920 works and 1,170 child ratings, set-based track ancestry reduced a five-run median from 127 ms to 23 ms with identical evidence for all 240 roots. The copied account's final background rebuild, including shortlist warming, took 10.3 seconds. An alternative shortlist join produced the same 2,690 IDs but required more than twice as many PostgreSQL buffer hits, so the existing join stayed.
- In a separate synthetic worker scaling trial, 25,000 training titles took approximately 0.39 seconds of in-process training versus 0.34 seconds previously. Peak process RSS including the fixture was about 177 MiB versus 149 MiB. Scoring 72 already-hydrated candidates took 1.01 ms versus 0.96 ms. This measures training on already hydrated inputs. The database-copy benchmark measures a complete request with its own service overhead.
- Focused regression suites cover new and stored references, creator weighting, per-medium selection, temporal and franchise evidence, child credits, TMDB ingestion, scoped friend evidence, cache invalidation and publication races. The historical source register has an unrelated local roadmap edit. Its isolated check uses the recorded roadmap and the current implementation sources.

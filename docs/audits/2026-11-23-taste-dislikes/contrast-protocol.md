# Personal dislike contrast controls

This protocol is fixed before examining the two new controls' holdouts. Both compare the sealed graded movie/show predictions on the same target IDs, masks and forecast days. All labels used by a new control are the viewer's verified direct stars. A target or an entire held-out fold contributes no training label. The control defers to the graded score on already represented franchises, masked ratings and unsupported cases.

1. Compare the two nearest unrelated disliked families' metadata similarities with the two closest other rated families. This checks a title's particular evidence on both sides. The average similarities enter a signed contrast; proximity to just one liked or disliked franchise cannot create extra independent examples.
2. Fit a class-balanced, L2-regularised logistic content classifier on the viewer's previous works. Split at 2½ stars and use all other direct ratings as counterexamples. Families contribute equal weight within each class. This tests whether a learned combination of features discriminates dislike after removing the gross imbalance in training counts. Its class-balanced output is a diagnostic rank; it is not a probability of personal dislike.

Use identical per-family-normalised vectors for each control, with fixed weights (1 each for genres/themes/tags, 0.8 for director/writer/creator, 0.65 cast, 0.5 network/studio). Weight each observed ID by the logarithm of inverse frequency in descriptive metadata; no other viewers or audience opinions enter. Exclude nonselection, franchise ID, title identity, and target history from the predictors. The classifier uses fixed penalty 0.03 on a unit-norm vector. A score requires two disliked and two counterexample families; four inner family partitions supply withheld validation examples.

Within each outer training set, protect favourites by comparing all candidate rejection thresholds on the independent inner family predictions. Require at least two independently caught disliked families, zero rejected five-star families, at most one ≥4-star family for each four caught disliked families, and a positive net gain after charging four per liked mistake and one per middling mistake. Choose the largest gain, with fewer rejections breaking ties. Only lower a graded score if its contrast exceeds a qualifying threshold: estimate its new fit from the flagged inner families' actual star utilities, shrunk by one 3½-star pseudo-observation, and require the estimate itself to be below 50. Ineligible cohorts retain the original predictions, including abstentions. A green favourite removed counts as a loss even if another favourite crosses into green.

Report all registered 32 cohorts, baseline and candidate score agreement, full/weak/favourite pair ordering, below-neutral predictions among disliked and liked works, false greens, the exact number of paired green favourites retained/lost/gained, and means before/after for dislikes. A change requires recoverable low matches without extra false reds or lost green favourites in the other splits. This is a reused one-owner historical experiment. An eligible future prediction supplies stronger evidence.

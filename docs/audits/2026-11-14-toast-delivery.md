# Toast delivery and experimental presentation

This follow-up changes how Coast presents action feedback, notifications, and playback and job status messages. The updated source pins cover this change. The earlier performance and hosted companion measurements still describe the versions and scenarios recorded in their original receipts.

- **Queue and playback.** The queue change assigns a persistent notification to permanent service failures. Claim order, retry scheduling, connection checks and worker ownership were reviewed against the diff. Playback now sends skipped-track and queue-completion messages through shared feedback. The playback lifecycle and notification unit tests, and the PostgreSQL auth, queue and notification suites, exercise the affected boundaries.
- **Search and settings.** The search change publishes the refinement message through shared feedback and retains the existing cancellation and stale-intent tests. The notification preference is still read within the configuration snapshot. The settings, preview inventory and feature-boundary checks cover the updated controls and their declared props. The experiment defaults off and uses the existing policy write.
- **Toast demand.** The standard query omits the context column. The experiment batches context for at most 50 notices, reuses permission-scoped work and actor reads, loads the media card only when the front notice has artwork, and mounts one card. Temporary notices start a timer only while they are in front. Existing dismissal writes, bounded batching, and retry behavior remain covered by the lifetime and database suites.
- **Browser.** On the isolated live-data copy, Chromium exercised the policy write in both directions. Off showed three standard cards with no glass or stamps. On showed one `glassDark` card, advanced after a temporary notice expired, retained the next important notice beyond seven seconds, and advanced again on dismissal. The temporary QA account was removed and the policy restored.

These are functional and work-bound checks. Provider round trips, repeat playback, native-device rendering, and production latency were outside this pass. Earlier remaining-work and hosted companion boundaries still apply.

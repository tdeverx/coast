# Can one viewer’s choices predict enjoyment without ratings?

For the viewer, returning to a franchise predicted that the viewer liked its first watched film. Of the rated starting films in collections the viewer returned to, 57 of 59 received four or more stars. For collections they watched once, 29 of 36 did. **Neither group contained a film rated three stars or below.** This is useful evidence about known favourites. Using those choices to infer which unfamiliar films the viewer would dislike made the predictions worse.

We used only this account’s actions and title descriptions to fit three profiles. The first averages unit-length content vectors across previously watched titles. The second learns a difference between the features of titles the person returned to and those they watched once. The comparison titles were themselves watched, matched by opportunity size and year of first watch. Film training uses one opening film per collection and a separate-day return. Show training uses two qualifying dates in each of two seasons. A third profile requires at least three independent favourites and three explicit drops. With one show favourite, zero movie favourites and zero drops, it abstained throughout. Watchlist adds and pauses did not count as dislikes. No personal ratings, ratings of other people, provider audience scores or global popularity entered these fits.

Percentages are the share of correctly ordered pairs of titles rated at least four and at most three stars, counting ties half. Every title’s own decisions, episodes and opinions were withheld before scoring it. The contrast uses a regularised pairwise logistic fit on observed choices. Its blend can adjust a card by at most eight points. Term counts come exclusively from this viewer’s training works. Movie franchise identifiers were excluded from the contrast, since membership itself defines the target. The 2022 and 2024 controls rebuild Coast’s personal consumption evidence only from recorded watch times before each cutoff and use separately verified original rating dates. Metadata and catalogue membership use current sources. Historical release dates approximate availability and imported dates can describe retrospective logging. Across the five work splits, the blended show gain was 4.67 points, with a paired title-bootstrap 95% interval of +0.60 to +9.08. On later first watches the gain was 1.20 points, interval −15.97 to +20.81. Each 2022 cohort has just four weak titles. The later movie and show cohorts have two and one respectively. The 94.23% for the movie content average and 80% for the show contrast predict too few independent weak outcomes to use as a general result. The film work-split contrast regressed even when we held entire franchises out. Keep these candidates out of the card’s enjoyment score.

The primary literature supports learning profiles from personally observed actions but shows why the action’s meaning needs validation. Billsus and Pazzani explicitly distinguished several reasons a viewer could interrupt their news agent. Claypool and colleagues independently compared behavioural cues with reported interest and found they differed substantially in predictive value. Their cohorts were reading news and browsing the web. Coast’s tests above are the direct evidence for viewing habits.

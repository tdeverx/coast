# Within-genre comparisons for film and show traits

Before seeing the results, freeze the current selected forecasts and display map and test an adjustment based on other titles' outcomes. An account that usually rates films four stars can still systematically prefer or avoid a keyword, performer, creator or company relative to similar work.

For each held-out title, exclude its family, every member of its held fold for grouped tests, and dates on or after its rating day for chronological tests. Find eligible other works that have a particular stable provider keyword or credit and share at least half their genres with the target. For each such family, use its unique closest work to the target. Find that work’s unique closest same-medium comparison with at least half the same genres, without the tested trait, outside the other families carrying it. Similarity uses genre Jaccard twice plus provider keyword Jaccard once. Discard tied best matches, comparison families reused across pairs, and groups too small to compare.

Use at least three independent pairs. At least three quarters must rate the feature consistently better or worse than the control. Require the median rating of the actual feature carriers to be at least four stars before lifting a score or at most 3½ before lowering one. Shrink the median utility difference toward zero with six pairs of prior mass, weight by the agreement margin and cap the resulting correction at four points. A forecast cannot move beyond the observed median utility of the carriers.

Test separately the strongest eligible keyword, strongest eligible credit, agreement of both tested on disjoint families, and the positive direction of that last rule. Equal countervailing cues cancel. Keep selected corrections and existing red forecasts untouched. Count every change to a dislike or favourite, new red and green predictions, and title ordering within medium, genre and split. A change should preserve the existing eight red dislikes, avoid new false reds, and generalise beyond the full-history replay without relying on a specific title or a change to the display curve.

The first run showed that a keyword or credit whose carriers earn higher ratings is not necessarily a cue the current profile underestimates. For a follow-up, keep the sampling and thresholds and compare the carriers' out-of-sample prediction errors with the errors of matched controls. Require at least 75% of carrier errors to agree with that differential, and cap the difference by their median absolute directional error. Exclude the target and donor families from the donor fit and use only strictly earlier data in the dated replay. Compare keywords, credits, disjoint corroboration and positive credits only. This checks for incremental evidence rather than counting a known preference twice.
